SPI Code Window Layout

What the window looks like after the M2 layout, sweep-settings and shell passes: one top row that each pane owns its part of, a status capsule that says the line's state at a glance, flush panes that resize, a sidebar of pins, a Detail panel that reads at any width, and Settings for Readiness, Sweep and Appearance behind a gear.

The window used to spend 71 px on two status rows before any work showed, said when it last swept in three places, framed the centre as a floating card, fixed the Detail panel at 300 px and stated readiness in five full-width cards. The layout pass merged the rows, drew the panes flush and resizable within a width budget, and turned Setup into the Readiness section of a Settings dialog opened with ⌘,. After it, the top row still read as a sentence of states, dates and counts, both panel toggles sat at the far right from the sidebar they open, and the sidebar held permanent links to places, each with a value Today already shows.

The shell pass of 2026-11-13 (epic spicode-44k, prototype spicode-bx8) settles the shell drawn here. Each pane owns its part of one 44 px top row. A status capsule says the program's state at a glance: a check when fine, a count when not, and the sweep at its end. Each panel toggle sits by the pane it controls. The sidebar holds Find or ask, Today and one Pinned list, and its footer is a gear for Settings. Appearance offers a Neutral palette beside Warm.

The sweep-settings pass adds next-sweep feedback, the tray menu, the Today badge and notifications for new Interrupts or a program the app can no longer read. It adds no editable schedule, pause, quiet hours, notifications switch or sweep history. Service-page station cells, right-panel editor tabs, the Models section and the GitHub token override stay outside this page.

Top row

The window has one top row, 44 px tall, and each pane draws its own part of it from the window's top. The parts share one bottom rule, and the empty space in every part is a window drag area. Nothing runs under the row except, while a sweep runs, its progress line.

Targets

Each segment is one button, the capsule's height. Env opens the Environment place, Templates the Engineering system, and Forks the Line sheet, carrying ⌘L as its shortcut and the sheet as what it controls. Sync opens the Line sheet too, and the sweep opens its popover. Each segment's hover and accessible name carry the sentence behind its state and name the forks behind a count ("Forks: 2 stopped. Stopped: storage, indexer-queue"); Templates also names the target revision. A service's pin and its other actions are on its Line sheet row, which is also the keyboard path.

Windows

On Windows the row is the 44 px titleBarOverlay height and there are no window controls on the left, so the sidebar toggle starts the row. The caption buttons take about 140 px at the right of whichever part ends the row: the right panel's tabs when the panel is inline, else a pinned Line sheet's toolbar, else the centre's part. That part pads clear of them, so its toggle sits to their left. The fold steps measure the centre's part, so they need no separate threshold.

Panes and surfaces

Each pane is a full-height column that touches its neighbours at a 1 px line. Nothing inside a pane is boxed unless it is a separate object: tiles, chips and list rows keep their own borders, and a place no longer sits inside a card. The global rule that gives every <section> a border is replaced by an explicit card class; that rule is what turned the service page and every Setup row into cards.

Resizing

The sidebar, the right panel and a pinned Line sheet each have a drag edge on the line they share with their neighbour. The edge is an 8 px target straddling the 1 px divider; on hover, focus or drag the divider turns accent and the cursor becomes a column resize. The width a drag ends on is the pane's preferred width, saved and restored on the next launch on the same machine together with whether each pane was open or collapsed.

The preferred width is what the operator chose. The width budget below decides the displayed width, which can be narrower than preferred, down to the inline minimum, without changing what is saved. Each edge is a focusable separator that announces its current, minimum and maximum width; arrow keys move it 16 px and Shift with an arrow 64 px. While dragging, a pane stops at the width that leaves the centre 520 px; each arrow-key step counts as a finished drag.

Width budget

Panes fold by the width left for the centre, not by one window breakpoint. The centre keeps at least 520 px. When the panes at their preferred widths do not fit beside it, the window takes these steps in order and stops at the first that fits:

StepWhat happens
1Narrow. The right panel narrows toward 280 px, then the pinned Line sheet toward 320 px.
2Detail becomes a sheet. The right panel opens as a sheet over the right edge of the window at its preferred width, at most the window width less 48 px. The panes still inline go back to their preferred widths, then step 1's narrowing applies again to them.
3Line sheet stacks. A pinned Line sheet moves under the centre; the two split the height equally.
4Sidebar off-canvas. The sidebar slides away behind ⌘B and its toggle, which stays just after the window controls in the centre's part of the top row.

Bars are drawn to one scale, 0.58. Widths use the default preferred sizes; a pane resized wider moves each threshold by the same amount. The budget is re-decided on window resize, on opening or closing a pane, when a drag ends and on each arrow-key step. The window cannot be made narrower than 740 px, the last case drawn. Sheets open below the top row, close with Escape or their close button, and return focus to the control that opened them, as they do today. Today the only rule is the 980 px breakpoint, so at 1000 px the centre is left 468 px.

Selecting an item switches the panel to Detail even when Evidence was showing, and the panel toggle with nothing selected opens the sweep's Evidence, never an empty Detail.

Settings

Setup becomes the Readiness section of a Settings dialog that opens over the current place with ⌘, or from the gear in the sidebar footer, and closes with Escape or its close button without changing the place behind it. Its sections are Readiness, Sweep and Appearance, in that order. The dialog is at most 820 by 560 px and never closer than 24 px to the window edge; its body scrolls. Below 760 px of window width the left nav becomes a section picker in the dialog header.

Each tool is one row: name, a status chip, the reason or version, and an action on the right. Rows keep a fixed order whatever their state. Reasons wrap; nothing is cut off. A row that needs more than a line (a sign-in code, a token field, an install in progress, an error) grows a body under its first line. Rows that are ready have no button; Check all in the header checks every tool again, and the header says when it last checked.

The sweep-settings council decision of 2026-11-09 sets this section's behaviour. Schedule, What a sweep reads, GitHub budget and Notifications share one scrolling body. Notifications

SPI Code tells you when a sweep finds something new that needs you, or when it can no longer read the program. Routine sweeps are quiet. Your system's notification settings control sound and quiet hours.

On Windows the schedule says "when Windows wakes". Cadences are fixed at 5, 30 and 60 minutes to protect the GitHub budget and the stale-at-twice-the-interval rule (EV-02). No editable intervals, pause, quiet hours, per-source toggles or notifications switch: quitting is the pause, and OS Focus or Do Not Disturb own quiet hours. There is no sweep history page, per-source next-due time, percentage or ETA.

Sweep now

Sweep now reads every source, not just those due, through the same readiness gate and GitHub budget floor. A source the budget cannot cover keeps "waits for GitHub budget · after HH:MM". The 5-minute timer restarts from the end of the requested sweep. A second request within 60 seconds of a forced sweep joins as non-forced; the window disables the button in that interval. Nothing starts a second concurrent sweep.

Notifications after the first sweep

Only new Interrupts notify, coalesced to at most one notification per committed sweep, and never while the window is focused. Dedupe by item id and rung is in memory for this app run; the same item notifies again only when it worsens. After relaunch, the first committed snapshot sends one summary of unacknowledged Interrupts, only when the window is not focused. Acknowledging closes its banner, not the Today count.

Scheduler and contract

Desktop main posts sweep.request {} 15 seconds after resume or unlock when the last sweep is older than the interval. Settings, Evidence and the tray read the same schedule and progress; none infers a time or count from prose.

sweep.status is extended rather than adding an event: nullable nextSweepAt, schedule (the sweep interval and GitLab, Security and Releases cadences), and nullable, session-only lastSweep {sweep, startedAt, endedAt, outcome, requested, code?}. The host sends it on every scheduler transition and replays it on view.subscribe. sweep.request gains optional now: true; an empty payload stays Try again on the non-forced path through the readiness gate. Follow the protocol rules on PROTOCOL_VERSION. Last sweep and notification dedupe need no new durable store. The contract and host work is risk:high, as is notification delivery; the other sweep-settings work is risk:std.

Before any snapshot, a cancelled first sweep is stopped too, unless a new first sweep is already running. The capsule says "First sweep stopped"; opening sweep Evidence with nothing selected shows the same stopped panel as Today, with its reason, source list, Try again and only the Settings actions a Readiness fix allows. A cancelled sweep says "The sweep was cancelled." and uses the next attempt from sweep.status.nextSweepAt, since cancelled progress carries no retry time. A failed sweep uses nextAttemptAt. Neither promises an expired time; an unscheduled retry has no time. A new running first sweep shows its live list in both places, never the older stop or a waiting message.

A missing or signed-out glab is a Readiness fix. Failed Upstream GitLab and Upstream radar reads carry the existing signin code and offer Open Settings, where glab can be installed or signed in. No separate missing-tool stop code is needed: the code is reused, not replaced with other. Unrelated transport failures do not gain a Settings action from sign-in words in their reason.

Example values throughout. The rows' names are the Evidence tab's area names, and the second column is fixed copy per source. Progress is never stored: the host sends at most four updates a second and replays the latest to a window that subscribes late; the Sweep section above extends sweep.status with the schedule, not the source list. Every time and count comes from a field, never from prose: a cut source names the read kind from cutKind and counts from incomplete (cut reads of the total), says "rest read" only when other reads completed and none failed, and gives the complete-read count and safe cut detail on hover in Today and Evidence. A job log cut at its character limit reads "1 job log cut short; rest read"; its hover says "68 of 69 reads complete. Job log character limit: 64,000 of 92,438 characters read." A source where some reads failed while the rest read is partial, not failed: it counts the failed reads from incomplete.failed, and the next line is the host's failure sentence naming services by fleet id, never core's failure text; a source that read nothing stays failed with its real reason code. A put-off or throttled source's "after 09:10" or "Resumes at 08:04" comes from resumeAt, and the stopped sweep's "tries again at" from nextAttemptAt, the real retry time, not the budget reset in sweep.status.

Validation

The width budget is a pure function from window width, preferred widths and open panes to displayed widths and presentations, tested at the eight cases drawn above and at the thresholds either side of each step. The separators have component tests for pointer drag, arrow keys, double-click reset, the announced values, and a displayed width narrower than the saved one without the saved one changing. The Detail panel has a test that renders a long Related label at 280 px and asserts every value keeps at least 160 px.

The capsule's tests are over snapshots, not made-up tones: each segment classifies as fine, not fine with its worst count, or unread, and unknown, empty or missing station data, unknown rollout slots and an unread environment figure classify as unread; Sync appears at exactly 2 hours, inside it, and never past or unknown; the sweep tone puts failed over stale, incomplete or cut over fine; the progress line fills by sources settled over sources and is absent with no progress; the popover's source states and its short next-sync form; and the three segments' targets with Forks' shortcut. The pins store is tested for its seed, a kept empty list, a corrupt value and the order across a restart; the pinned service's state word over snapshots; the gear's dot on the Readiness decision; and the palette choice for stored, missing and unknown values. The packaged smoke holds one top row, every pane header at the window's top and in line, and the 48 px drag span at 740 px. Glyphs, words in the pills, hover copy and layout are judged from captures. The first sweep has tests for every row state and copy line, Try again and Open Settings in the stopped state, hand-over only on view.snapshot, each place's one line before the first snapshot, both sweeping states of the sweep segment and where the popover's Evidence lands, and the tray's "Today is ready". Settings has tests for ⌘, and the footer gear, focus moving in and returning on close, the first-launch open over the Watch gate, the banner turning green, and each row state drawn above. The packaged screenshot suite captures every surface, Settings and the first sweep (running with a failed source, stopped, and the top row during a later sweep) included, at 1440, 900 and 740 px in light and dark on macOS and Windows; those captures are the maintainer's visual check against this page.

The sweep-settings pass checks ST-07's contract extension and protocol rules, forced all-source reads with the readiness gate and budget floor, timer restart and cooldown joins, resume and unlock, and no persistence of session status. Window tests cover every drawn Sweep now state, expired times, the moved budget and link, and the 740 px column fold. Desktop tests cover tray actions and committed dot tone, the badge, coalesced notifications, focus suppression, rung dedupe and relaunch, both outage triggers and reset on commit, click targets and every never-notify case. Its document-only bead renders this page in headless Chrome at 1440 and 740 px in light and dark; it does not run a packaged smoke.
