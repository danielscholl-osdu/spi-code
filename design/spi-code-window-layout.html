SPI Code Window Layout

What the window looks like after the M2 layout, sweep-settings and shell passes: one top row that each pane owns its part of, holding the title and a pin control at the centre's right end, flush panes that resize, a fixed status block above removable pins and a sweep beside the footer gear, rows and popovers that open in place instead of a side panel, and Settings for Readiness, Sweep and Appearance behind a gear.

The window used to spend 71 px on two status rows before any work showed, said when it last swept in three places, framed the centre as a floating card, fixed a detail panel at 300 px and stated readiness in five full-width cards. The layout pass merged the rows, drew the panes flush and resizable within a width budget, and turned Setup into the Readiness section of a Settings dialog opened with ⌘,. After it, the top row still read as a sentence of states, dates and counts, both panel toggles sat at the far right from the sidebar they open, and the sidebar held permanent links to places, each with a value Today already shows.

Each pane owns its part of one 44 px top row. The sidebar toggle sits by the sidebar, and a place or service declares its pin control at the right end of the centre's part. Appearance offers a Neutral palette beside Warm.

The Today and places pass of 2026-11-17 (spi-code-today-and-places.md) removed the right panel outside a thread. The window is the sidebar and the centre, plus the Line sheet when docked. A Today row opens under itself, a place opens a popover on what was clicked, and Evidence is a page. The right panel stays as a thread's context pane at M3.

The sidebar leads with Find or ask and a fixed block: Today, Line, Environment and Template. Pinned follows with only removable pins. The status marks keep their meanings: Line and Template carry outlined counts, Environment a check, wrench or refresh icon, and Upstream a clock near the next sync. Hover never displaces a mark or a service's word. The footer holds the gear and sweep; an Undo band sits just above it.

The sweep-settings pass adds next-sweep feedback, the tray menu, the Today badge and notifications for new Interrupts or a program the app can no longer read. It adds no editable schedule, pause, quiet hours, notifications switch or sweep history. Service-page station cells, a thread panel's editor tabs, the Models section and the GitHub token override stay outside this page.

Top row

The window has one top row, 44 px tall, and each pane draws its own part of it from the window's top. The parts share one bottom rule, and the empty space in every part is a window drag area. Nothing runs under the row except, while a sweep runs, its progress line.

Windows

On Windows the row is the 44 px titleBarOverlay height and there are no window controls on the left, so the sidebar toggle starts the row. The caption buttons take about 140 px at the right of whichever part ends the row: a docked Line sheet's toolbar, else the centre's part, or in a thread the right panel's tabs when that panel is inline. That part pads clear of them, so its end controls sit to their left. When the docked Line sheet ends the row, its Undock and Close stay whole and clear of the caption buttons; as room runs short the "· 13 forks" count drops first, then the "Line" title truncates with an ellipsis. The fold steps measure the centre's part, so they need no separate threshold.

Status on the sidebar rows

The program's state sits at the ends of the rows that open what explains it. Line, Environment and Template keep their marks in the fixed block; Upstream keeps its clock wherever the operator moves its removable pin, and an unpinned Upstream draws it nowhere. A mark is a number or icon. Today keeps its filled count, the count that means act; the status counts are outlined.

The dot when the sidebar is hidden

While the sidebar is hidden or off-canvas its toggle carries one dot: error when an Interrupt is open, a status mark is error or the sweep is bad; otherwise warning when a mark is warning or the sweep is warn. Error wins across sources. An unread sweep adds nothing, so with nothing else read there is no dot. The collapsed Pinned header reads its own rows' marks and service words: stopped is error, waiting warning, no word read gives none. It does not read the fixed block. Neither dot is a count. The sidebar's Today count is the members on Blocked and Waiting on you, while this dot, the dock badge and the tray read Interrupts; whether they should read the same members is an open question for the checkpoint (spicode-djg.29).

The fixed block and Pinned

Today, Line, Environment and Template form one block with no header. They have no grip, context menu or pin control anywhere, including Find, and never reorder. Pinned follows, collapsible and hidden when empty, with Upstream, Releases and Security on first run and then the operator's removable pins. Right click, long press, Shift-F10 or Menu key on a Pinned row opens Open, Move up, Move down, a separator and Unpin from sidebar. Alt+Up and Alt+Down move a focused pin. Drag inserts only within Pinned; dropping on the fixed block or outside the list does nothing. After Unpin, focus goes to the next row, else the previous, else the Pinned header, else the fixed block's last row, without a timed page query.

The stored list uses spi-code.sidebar.pins.v3. A readable v3 list wins; a corrupt v3 gives the seed and never falls back. With no v3, migrate v2 by removing sheet:line, place:environment and place:engineering-system, retaining the operator's other pins and order, including removed seed places. Otherwise read legacy services after the seed; corrupt older entries give seed. Migration writes v3 once and leaves the old keys untouched. Refused reads give seed with window-only writes; a refused migration write keeps the migrated list here and retries next launch.

One control and its feedback

The top row's 24 px control, Find's result-end control and the Line sheet's after-name control share the same hollow, filled and pin-off states (22 px in lists, 14 px glyph). Its action name and title include the item: "Pin partition to sidebar (⌘⇧P)" or "Unpin partition from sidebar (⌘⇧P)", with Ctrl+Shift+P on Windows; no aria-pressed with a changing name. A click keeps the new glyph until pointer or focus leaves. None on Today, a release month or the fixed places. Find toggles in place, ⌘↩ opens and pins without unpinning, and ⌘⇧P toggles the highlighted result. Outside Find the shortcut toggles the open centre place or service, except under another dialog or the overlay Line sheet; shell state decides the exclusion.

Every unpin offers "Unpinned partition · Undo" for 8 s. Undo or ⌘Z (Ctrl+Z) returns the pin to its old index. A pin appends and flashes the new row's accent background for about 1 s when Pinned is visible and open; otherwise "Pinned partition · Undo" offers to unpin. The newest notice replaces the last. The sidebar band sits immediately above the footer; with the sidebar hidden or off-canvas it becomes a notice at the window's bottom left. Its status region is mounted empty before filling, Undo is a keyboard button, and hover or focus pauses the timer. The shown notice owns ⌘Z, inside Find too, until another key; elsewhere normal Undo remains. Sidebar-owned state tracks whether Pinned is visible. Changes and Undo update this window immediately even if best-effort storage writes fail; the words name the change and make no saved claim, and reload uses the last list storage accepted.

Panes and surfaces

Each pane is a full-height column that touches its neighbours at a 1 px line. Nothing inside a pane is boxed unless it is a separate object: tiles, chips and list rows keep their own borders, and a place no longer sits inside a card. The global rule that gives every <section> a border is replaced by an explicit card class; that rule is what turned the service page and every Setup row into cards.

Resizing

The sidebar, a docked Line sheet and, in a thread, the right panel each have a drag edge on the line they share with their neighbour. The edge is an 8 px target straddling the 1 px divider; on hover, focus or drag the divider turns accent and the cursor becomes a column resize. The width a drag ends on is the pane's preferred width, saved and restored on the next launch on the same machine together with whether each pane was open or collapsed.

Width budget

Panes fold by the width left for the centre, not by one window breakpoint. The centre keeps at least 520 px. When the panes at their preferred widths do not fit beside it, the window takes these steps in order and stops at the first that fits. Outside a thread there is no right panel and it takes no width, so step 2 cannot apply; step 1 still narrows a docked Line sheet toward 320 px, then steps 3 and 4 follow. Only the panel's narrowing and its sheet need a thread (M3).

The budget is re-decided on window resize, on opening or closing a pane, when a drag ends and on each arrow-key step. The window cannot be made narrower than 740 px, the last case drawn. Sheets open below the top row, close with Escape or their close button, and return focus to the control that opened them. Outside a thread the same function gives the panel zero width: at 1440 px with Line docked the centre keeps 848 px, at 1100 px Line narrows to 348 px inline and the centre keeps 520 px, at 1000 px Line stacks under the centre, and at 740 px the sidebar goes off-canvas.

Outside a thread nothing opens to the side. Settings

Setup becomes the Readiness section of a Settings dialog that opens over the current place with ⌘, or from the gear in the sidebar footer, and closes with Escape or its close button without changing the place behind it. Its sections are Readiness, Sweep and Appearance, in that order. The dialog is at most 820 by 560 px and never closer than 24 px to the window edge; its body scrolls. Below 760 px of window width the left nav becomes a section picker in the dialog header.

Each tool is one row: name, a status chip, the reason or version, and an action on the right. Rows keep a fixed order whatever their state. Reasons wrap; nothing is cut off. A row that needs more than a line (a sign-in code, a token field, an install in progress, an error) grows a body under its first line. Rows that are ready have no button; Check all in the header checks every tool again, and the header says when it last checked.

Notifications

SPI Code tells you when a sweep finds something new that needs you, or when it can no longer read the program. Routine sweeps are quiet. Your system's notification settings control sound and quiet hours.

On Windows the schedule says "when Windows wakes". Cadences are fixed at 5, 30 and 60 minutes to protect the GitHub budget and the stale-at-twice-the-interval rule (EV-02). No editable intervals, pause, quiet hours, per-source toggles or notifications switch: quitting is the pause, and OS Focus or Do Not Disturb own quiet hours. There is no sweep history page, per-source next-due time, percentage or ETA.

Sweep now

Sweep now reads every source, not just those due, through the same readiness gate and GitHub budget floor. A source the budget cannot cover keeps "waits for GitHub budget · after HH:MM". The 5-minute timer restarts from the end of the requested sweep. A second request within 60 seconds of a forced sweep joins as non-forced; the window disables the button in that interval. Nothing starts a second concurrent sweep.

After a sweep with a failed source, or one cut by failed reads, the button reads Read again, in Settings and in Evidence alike. It sends the non-forced request (sweep.request without now): every due source is read, and a source whose last read failed or was cut by failures is due, so GitHub issues and PRs is read again while Upstream GitLab, Upstream radar, Security alerts and Release lines wait for their interval unless they failed too. A scheduled sweep retries a failed source the same way. Read again keeps Sweep now's disabled rules, and a cut by a page or size limit alone keeps Sweep now.

Only new Interrupts notify, coalesced to at most one notification per committed sweep, and never while the window is focused. Scheduler and contract

Desktop main posts sweep.request {} 15 seconds after resume or unlock when the last sweep is older than the interval. Settings, Evidence and the tray read the same schedule and progress; none infers a time or count from prose.

sweep.status is extended rather than adding an event: nullable nextSweepAt, schedule (the sweep interval and GitLab, Security and Releases cadences), and nullable, session-only lastSweep {sweep, startedAt, endedAt, outcome, requested, code?}. The host sends it on every scheduler transition and replays it on view.subscribe. sweep.request gains optional now: true; an empty payload stays Try again on the non-forced path through the readiness gate. Follow the protocol rules on PROTOCOL_VERSION. Last sweep and notification dedupe need no new durable store. The contract and host work is risk:high, as is notification delivery; the other sweep-settings work is risk:std.

Before any snapshot, a cancelled first sweep is stopped too, unless a new first sweep is already running. The footer sweep says "First sweep stopped"; opening sweep Evidence with nothing open shows the same stopped panel as Today, with its reason, source list, Try again and only the Settings actions a Readiness fix allows. A cancelled sweep says "The sweep was cancelled." and uses the next attempt from sweep.status.nextSweepAt, since cancelled progress carries no retry time. A failed sweep uses nextAttemptAt. Neither promises an expired time; an unscheduled retry has no time. A new running first sweep shows its live list in both places, never the older stop or a waiting message.

A missing or signed-out glab is a Readiness fix. Failed Upstream GitLab and Upstream radar reads carry the existing signin code and offer Open Settings, where glab can be installed or signed in. No separate missing-tool stop code is needed: the code is reused, not replaced with other. Unrelated transport failures do not gain a Settings action from sign-in words in their reason.

Example values throughout. The rows' names are the Evidence page's area names, and the second column is fixed copy per source. Progress is never stored: the host sends at most four updates a second and replays the latest to a window that subscribes late; the Sweep section above extends sweep.status with the schedule, not the source list. Every time and count comes from a field, never from prose: a cut source names the read kind from cutKind and counts from incomplete (cut reads of the total), says "rest read" only when other reads completed and none failed, and gives the complete-read count and safe cut detail on hover in Today and Evidence. A job log cut at its character limit reads "1 job log cut short; rest read"; its hover says "68 of 69 reads complete. Job log character limit: 64,000 of 92,438 characters read." A source where some reads failed while the rest read is partial, not failed: it counts the failed reads from incomplete.failed, and the next line is the host's failure sentence naming services by fleet id, never core's failure text; a source that read nothing stays failed with its real reason code. A put-off or throttled source's "after 09:10" or "Resumes at 08:04" comes from resumeAt, and the stopped sweep's "tries again at" from nextAttemptAt, the real retry time, not the budget reset in sweep.status.

The popover shell has tests in components/popover/popover.test.tsx (spicode-djg.11): placement under, flipped above, on the roomier side with a height that fits, and clamped at each margin; focus moving in and back to the anchor; Esc closing before any earlier window listener; and any outside click closing it except on the footer sweep. components/opened-body/opened-body.test.tsx (spicode-djg.8) tests the one body for every kind, the row's own pill never repeated, and that no button renders (the Z7 and R1 gate).
