Security at fleet scale: see where the alerts pile up before reading a row

One figure leads: each lane is a bar on one scale, and a rule or package is a block in it sized by its alerts. Below it the chosen lane reads two ways, by rule or by repository, and both get longer as the fleet grows, never wider.

Draft 10 is draft 7 after two rounds of a four-seat council, a wording review and a render check; the notes below the window say what changed. Every count is from the sweep of 20 Nov 2026, 10:02; the paths, alert and PR numbers, advisories and versions are made up. Click a lane, a block, a row, a tick or a repository. The design

  • One figure leads. The three lanes are bars on one scale, so 141 upstream alerts look three and a half times the size of 40 of ours. Each block is a rule or package, widest first; a lane’s smallest share one “N more” block at its end. In Shipped SPI code every rule with a high or critical alert keeps its own block, however small.
  • The lane is the page’s one filter. Clicking a lane name chooses it. Clicking a block chooses its lane and opens that row.
  • By rule and By repository are two views of the chosen lane. A rule row carries a strip with one tick per repository; a repository row carries its bar, its count, its total across the whole page and its top rules. Repositories with nothing share one line.
  • Colour says whose move it is. Ours is blue, upstream takes the upstream role’s magenta, and tests, tooling and settings are grey. Severity is never a hue, and amber and red still mean Waiting and Blocked only.
  • Every figure can be checked against GitHub. The line above the headline counts everything open by scanner, and each repository shows its total across the page.
  • The opened row is draft 6’s, unchanged.

What changed in drafts 8 to 10

  • Severity is solid against outlined. Draft 7 used a tint for medium and low, which measured about 1.8 to 2.1:1 against the surface. A lower-severity mark is now a pale fill with an outline in the lane’s own colour, and every lane colour is at least 3:1 on the surface and on a raised row in all four palette and theme pairs. Ink only is the same encoding in ink.
  • The bars are to scale. A block had an 8px floor, so the 26 tests blocks filled their bar whatever their counts. A rule or package now gets its own block when it is at least 4% of the longest lane. The rest of a lane share one block, with a divider at each rule’s boundary, labelled with their alert count; its tooltip lists them, and clicking it chooses the lane.
  • A block’s label is its count, on a chip. It reads the same over solid and outlined, in every palette. It sits at the block’s end, so it never covers the solid part, which starts at the left. It is the alert count alone, or nothing where it does not fit; on a block that is part solid it must fit in the outlined part. Names stay off the bars: a name that shows only where a block is wide enough says nothing, and the rows below name every rule. A block’s name is in its tooltip and accessible name. Every number in a bar is an alert count, so a bar’s numbers add up to its total.
  • A tick’s height grows with its alert count (by square root, on one scale for the lane), and its solid part is that repository’s share of high or critical, from the baseline up, and the line under the strip names the repository that holds most of a rule: “3 forks · 79 in crs-conversion”. A rule in one repository names it. Pointing at a tick names its repository and count under that strip. The strip takes focus: arrow keys step through its repositories and Enter opens the rule with that one marked.
  • One name per rule. The rows, By repository and each block’s tooltip all use the scanner’s own title. Draft 7’s short names came from a hand-written table the sweep cannot produce.
  • Fewer words above the first row. The sentence under the figure is gone: the strip’s line now says what it said. The legend is two swatches, plus Not read when a list was not read. The summary line drops the lane name and total the figure already shows.
  • “Lane” is no longer on the screen. It is this design’s word, not the operator’s.
  • Not read is hatched. A hatched tick, a hatched stub and a hatched swatch; beside a repository’s name it is still ⌀. The stub keeps the same room in every lane, so the bars stay on one scale.
  • ≥ sits only on a number an unread list could change. A rule’s total, its fork count and its age carry it; a count from a list that was read does not. A row names its main repository only when every list the rule draws on was read.
  • By repository lists what drives the bar. Top rules are ordered by alert count. In an opened repository a rule is a pill that opens it across the program. A rule with many alerts there is listed by file, each file with its own pills or a count pill that opens in place.
  • Narrow windows keep the fix. Below 900px the fix, age and fix PR words move to a line under the rule’s name.
  • A clean lane says so once, in its bar.

Found in the data

  • 59 of 82 dependency alerts sit beside a fix PR that was closed unmerged. Only 2 Dependabot PRs are open, both in crs-conversion. Each rule row now says how many closed fix PRs it has.
  • The upstream lane is mostly one file. 79 of the 93 User-controlled data in arithmetic alerts are in crs-conversion, 60 of them in YARrowBramble.java. Its block is the widest on the page and By repository shows it as one long bar, so nobody mistakes 141 for a fleet-wide problem.
  • One alert opened in the last seven days. “New this week” stays a phrase on the row, not a chart.

Proposed palette change

  • Three lane tokens beside the chart series: --lane-spi blue (#2a78d6 light, #3987e5 dark), --lane-upstream equal to --upstream, and --lane-tests a mid grey per palette. The values are draft 7’s.
  • Each lane colour measures 3.0:1 or more against the surface and against a raised row in Neutral and Warm, light and dark (the lowest is the tests grey on a Warm light raised row). The pale fill inside an outline is the lane colour mixed 16% into the surface and carries no meaning by itself.
  • A label on a block is the palette’s strongest text colour on a chip of its surface colour, so no label depends on a lane colour for contrast.
  • Two visual-design rules change. “Severity stays weight, never colour” holds: severity is solid against outlined, never a hue. The upstream role widens from upstream origin marks to findings in upstream code, which arrive the same way.
  • The blue sits near the work role. Work only marks refreshes and running sweeps, which never share a mark with this page’s charts.

For the build

  • The figure, the strip and By repository need no sweep field beyond draft 6’s: they all come from each alert’s repository, path, lane and severity. The one-line rule help and CWE come from two more, rule.full_description and rule.tags, and an opened rule shows neither when the scanner gives none.
  • Every mark splits the same way: the solid part is the share of high or critical alerts, from a block’s left, a tick’s baseline or a count bar’s start, and the rest is a 16% mix of the lane colour into the surface inside an outline of the lane colour. No mark has a fixed middle step.
  • The figure is on one scale across the three lanes. The Alerts column and the strip ticks are scaled to the chosen lane only, its largest rule and its largest repository count, because one lane’s rows show at a time.
  • The block order within a lane is by alert count; the rule list keeps draft 6’s order (worst severity, then how many are high, then count).
  • The figure and the strip have text equivalents: each block’s accessible name is its title, count and severity, and each strip’s is its repositories and counts.
  • Names on the screen are the scanner’s titles. There is no short-name table to maintain.
  • Draft 6’s other findings stand, including spicode-asl (core takes a code-scanning group’s reach from its first alert).
