spi-code · window information design · council review, six rounds

SPI Code Factory Line

An agent workbench for the SPI program. Work happens in threads, the factory line stays in view as a strip and a sheet you pull up, and the window interrupts you only when a decision is yours.

The current window reads like a transcript of the sweep, and the first revision of this page made it a better dashboard. A service page shows about 520 words. The revised Today packed a 13 × 6 grid, three bands and a queue into one view, which is busier than the rib console it replaces. It still asked the operator to visit pages and coordinate the work mentally. Threads were annotations on those pages.

This revision makes the thread the place work happens. The window has three layers. The strip, the window's top row, always shows the line's state. The centre holds either a short brief (Today), a place such as a service, or a thread. The right panel holds context for whatever the centre shows. Attention has four tiers decided by core, and only a decision that's yours interrupts. Watchers and campaign threads notice problems and draft the exact approval card. The operator decides, in one place per card.

Some things this doesn't change or provide. Every write still needs its own card (AU-03 to AU-10), and no agent approves anything. Lane occupancy, collision attribution, seen-state and due times are new data, marked where they appear. Sequencing across forks must be enforced in the stack's workflows, because the app can't stop jobs GitHub starts on its own.

The window

The window is T3 Code's shell. The sidebar holds work. The centre holds one thing: a brief, a place or a thread. The right panel is closed until the centre has context worth showing. One strip runs across the top on every surface, so the line is never more than a glance away.

The mockups on this page draw what each surface says, not how the window is laid out. Their fixed sidebar and right-panel widths, the title bar and strip as two rows, the place name and sweep line in the title bar, and Setup as a sidebar destination are superseded by the window layout: one top row that ends with the freshness field, flush panes that resize within a width budget, and Setup as the Readiness section of a Settings dialog. For layout, panes, the top row and Settings, the window layout wins over this page.

The top row

The strip is the window's top row on every surface. It reads Environment, Templates and Forks, then next sync and freshness on the right, so what every prove depends on comes first. Each state is one word when it is fine and a count of what is not when it isn't. No mark needs a legend, and nothing reads "n of m".

  • A fine state is a word on a light green tint; a count is in amber or red. The field's name stays in plain ink. The tints hold in both themes.
  • Hover and the accessible name carry the sentence behind the state and name the forks behind a count ("Stopped: storage, indexer-queue"); Templates also names the target revision. The maintenance length is the open hold's, from the Held lane core sends.
  • A field core couldn't read reads "not read yet" in plain ink: the environment's figure unread, the rollout's slots unknown, or the forks' stations unread. It never reads Ready, Current or Flowing.
  • Each field opens the place that explains it. Today draws no since row and no place marks in its band to repeat these states.
  • There are no service pips. A service is reached from the Line sheet, a place or ⌘K, and pinned from a place header, a Line sheet row or ⌘K (PS-17).
  • The lane's holder and the waiting cards have no field yet. In this design the top row has no field for the lane's holder when one is borrowing (M3) or the count of approval cards waiting (M4); spicode-cp0.1 decides where they go.

Next sync and the freshness field are unchanged. The window layout draws how the row folds and wraps.

Today: the daily briefing

Today is the operator's daily briefing on the whole program. It reads in the order a briefing is given: what needs you, what is coming from upstream, how the system stands place by place, the forming release, then what is moving and what can wait. Places are the drill-down: every row on Today opens the place, or the item, that owns it, and Today states each fact once. Time isn't Today's spine. There's no "due by", no "before 18:00" group and no clock rail; a time appears only as a fact about an event: "failed 13 h ago", "4 commits at the 18:00 sync", "green since 11-09 22:27".

Top to bottom: the verdict sentence, only when the band is empty; a caveat band when a source failed; the attention band, only when an Interrupt or in-system work asks for you, led by Stops the line when the shared environment is stopped; On the horizon, what is coming from upstream GitLab, on every morning; four place cards; then Releases, about two-thirds of the width, beside In motion and Can wait. Today lifts the centre's 960 px content cap and uses the full centre width; the window layout states the exception.

  • Today says nothing the top row already says. There is no since row or legend under a headline and no place marks above Needs you; the environment, template and fork states are on the top row, and the place cards below carry each place's mark.
  • The verdict is core's briefing.verdict: one sentence built from the tier counts, the environment's state and the stopped forks, naming both origins when both ask for you. It is drawn only when the band is empty, because the band already says what needs you. When the environment is in maintenance it leads: "The environment has been in maintenance for 2 d 13 h, and nothing is running to clear it.", or names the run that is clearing it, and the other Interrupts follow as "n other things need you". A hold whose start core doesn't know leads without a length; a hold core can't read isn't mentioned. The place cards agree with it. The date line carries the date only; freshness stays on the top row.
  • The attention band holds every Interrupt, whatever its origin, and the in-system work that asks for you, and is drawn only when it has some. It has no border or background of its own. Stops the line leads it when the shared environment is stopped and needs you (below); then Needs you, every other Interrupt whole and in rung order, never folded, with a "⇣ n upstream need you first" chip on its heading line that scrolls to the horizon; Decisions waiting beside it, the in-system Surface items grouped once per core theme, drawn only with items; then Failing, TEST items and GATE items whose check is red, folded by shared evidence so one lane failure is one line.
  • Stops the line is drawn above Needs you when the open hold in the environment's Held lane names an Interrupt item. Every prove borrows the shared environment, so its stop comes first. The block gives how long ("2 d 13 h") and since when, the environment reading's headline as its title, which opens the Environment place, the item's cause as Why and its next step as Next, the forks or work it holds up as a chip when core sends one, and the item's verb, which selects it in Detail. Needs you then lists the rest, headed "Needs you · services" when every remaining item is a service's. A hold with no item, an item that isn't an Interrupt, or a Held lane that wasn't read draws no block, and the item stays in Needs you. The Today count still counts every Interrupt.
  • Each group holds one tier, and each item has one home. Interrupt: Needs you, whatever its origin, except an Interrupt that is a stop of the shared environment, which is drawn in Stops the line above it and still counted in the Today count; an upstream Interrupt's horizon card also carries ✕. Surface: its horizon card when it comes from upstream, Decisions waiting when it's in your system. Ambient: Failing in the band, or In progress in In motion. Background: a place card's count, or Can wait. Today never re-tiers an item, and heading counts are item counts ("Failing · 3 in 2").
  • A place card describes its place, not its items. Its mark is the place's highest tier in the kit's tier marks (✕ ! ◐ ○), named in words on hover ("Background: nothing asks you today"). Its figure takes the reading's tone in ink, so Security's 28 shows in warn ink beside ○. When the mark isn't ○, a reason chip says why. ⌀ joins the figure when an area the card reads, from core's briefing.places[].areas, was cut or not read, with core's shortened qualifier as its caveat line. A "new" chip shows when the place has items new since you last looked. Upstream has no card: On the horizon is its summary, and Releases has no card because the Releases block is its summary.
  • Releases shows all 13 released versions, filled column-first and never folded. The released version comes from services[].releases[]; an open release PR's proposed version follows an arrow labelled on hover as the open release PR, whose accessible name reads "released 1.4.1, proposed 1.4.2". The platform line shows the stack, the template and core-lib-azure's spread across the 13, because one version would hide that services build on different library versions. The unreleased bar counts what the open release PRs carry, by kind, summing to the total; Notable names the one or two most material changes and the services they reach. A body that wasn't read reads "not read", never 0. Release history stays on Releases.
  • In motion is the In progress run, ROLLOUT, GATE, PORT and ENV items in core's order: the rollout's next merges as chips, and each waiting PR as an item row. Work that is moving isn't trouble, so it lives with the state, not in the band. Can wait is chips for the Background items no place card counts and the services with nothing waiting ("entitlements and file: no sync, gate or release waiting"), and opens the Can wait list in Detail with why each can wait.

Calm is the goal state, so Today is designed for the calm morning and grows for the bad one. On the horizon

Everything on Today belongs to one of two origins, told apart at a glance by position and one mark. In your system: the SPI forks, their PRs, the environment, the template and the releases. From upstream: commits made in upstream GitLab that haven't fully landed in the forks, whether queued for the next sync or held in open sync PRs, and merged upstream provider changes not yet ported. Upstream origin has one owner colour, the visual design's upstream role, and one inbound glyph, ⇣. They mark the horizon block, every upstream chip and an upstream wait's slot, and nothing else; colour is never the only signal.

  • The block is Upstream's summary on Today, the way the Releases block is Releases'. It sits full width under the band and above the place cards on every morning, because upstream always has a horizon. Its heading opens the Upstream place and takes ⌀ when the radar was cut.
  • The conveyor runs in three stages: the next sync, the open sync PRs, then your forks. Each stage shows its count and its fork chips from core's horizon stages; 18:00 is a fact on its stage. A fork whose sync is stopped or halted won't land at the next sync, wherever it appears: it moves to an uncounted "won't land" row under its stage, as a broken chip, so the heading counts exactly the chips above it. At 740 px the conveyor compacts to one line ("8 at 18:00 → 69 in 5 PRs → 4 current") and the stages expand on click.
  • What's coming is one card per upstream change core groups by theme: a kind glyph (toolchain ⇡, halt ⏸), the change as a token, the forks it reaches with their stage (a PR badge when a sync PR holds it, the time when it's queued), and the verb. A card that holds a Surface item carries ! and its verb as a button ("Absorb ›", "Pre-classify ›", "Move pin ›"). A change that needs nothing is a quiet card; it says it lands on its own only when core confirms a delivery state, and a held card says "held". The sub-heading counts both: "4 need you before they land · 1 to watch".
  • Ports owed, upstream provider changes not yet ported, are a per-fork segmented bar in the upstream tint at low saturation, with the figure carrying the number.
  • Upstream marks elsewhere. Apart from an upstream Interrupt in Needs you, whose horizon card also carries ✕, the band holds only in-system work. A sync PR is in your system as a PR but its content is upstream, so it carries the ⇣ chip in In motion and is counted on the conveyor's PR stage. On the Line card, a slot whose wait is upstream draws in the upstream colour and the card adds a "⇣ n on the horizon" chip, counted from the same join core builds for the cards. Upstream-owned findings are already in the forks, so "232 upstream-owned" carries no ⇣.

Words and visuals

Today is read at a glance, not read through. A word stays only when it carries something nothing else can: a decision, an action (the verb), the title of a change, or a caveat. So the words left are the headline, the Needs you title and verb, a decision's cause and verb, the failing check's name, the Notable titles and the caveats. Counts, states, progress, membership and time become marks, slots, bars and chips from the visual design's kit. The sentence a visual replaces becomes its hover and accessible name, from the same core field ("Sync Upstream failed on storage at 18:27"), and Detail keeps the full text. Every visual draws from a number or list core sends; the window computes nothing beyond layout.

Slot grammar: one meaning per slot shape, everywhere. Wherever Today draws a row of slots (the Line card's pips, the conveyor's fork chips, the rollout cells), a slot is in one of six states, told apart by shape as well as colour:

One column, and Detail open

At 740 px, and at 1440 with Detail open (a centre of about 850), Today is one column: the band if any, or the verdict when there is none, On the horizon, the place cards in a two- or three-wide grid, Releases, then In motion and Can wait.

  • Opening Detail never folds anything. Every group shows in full. When the column count changes, the selected row keeps its place on screen and the page scrolls around it.
  • Folds happen only at 740 px. Decisions waiting and each Ambient run show three lines, then an "n more ›" line that counts items like the headings and opens that group's full list in Detail. Folds are decided when the width changes; the selected row is kept among the lines shown, replacing the third, so the fold height stays fixed. Selecting never changes a fold, so Today under a Detail sheet doesn't move. Needs you never folds.
  • Releases keeps all 13 versions, in two columns at 740 and three at 850, filled column-first, and keeps its change lines.

The bad morning

Three Interrupts, and GitHub failed to read.

  • No verdict: the band holds the three Interrupts, so core's sentence ("3 things need you; 4 forks are stopped. GitHub wasn't read this sweep.") isn't drawn.
  • A caveat band, full width at the top: "GitHub read failed at 08:15; items from it may be missing. Evidence ›". It adds "Open Settings ›" only when the failure's code is one Readiness fixes, such as signin. The band covers the lists, so items, In motion and Can wait take no ⌀.

What goes

  • Today's three tiles, the "more" fold and the 75-word and five-item caps.
  • The deadline groups (Before the next sync) and the New group. New is a chip.
  • Grouping by when an item bites. Each group holds one tier.
  • The strip's "n due before 18:00" field. The strip keeps "next sync 18:00".
  • The strip's service pips and the stopped names. The top row says Forks and a count; the names are in its hover and on the Line sheet.

  • The verdict sentence on a morning with a band.
  • Release months on Today. Release history stays on Releases.

Each item keeps its P0, P1 or P2 rank as a chip. A pending card raised by a button is an Interrupt and is decided in Needs you (AU-10). A Surface item can still be snoozed until the next sync, and a composer anchored to the program closes the brief.

Before the first sweep finishes there is no brief yet. Today shows the sweep's own sources live from sweep.progress, in sweep order and in Evidence words, each with what it reads and its state, and hands over to the brief in place when view.snapshot lands. Places open one line saying when they can be read, and the freshness field reads "◐ sweeping · 9 of 12". If the first sweep stops, Today keeps the list and offers Try again and Open Settings. The window layout's first-sweep frame draws it.

The Line sheet

⌘L or the top row's Forks field opens the round 1 grid as a sheet over the centre. It closes back to where you were, or you can pin it beside a thread for a monitoring session. Rows that need nothing fold. A campaign chip filters the sheet to that campaign's forks and adds its wave column.

Places

A place is any anchor you can open: a service, the environment, the engineering system, a release month, a campaign. Places are Today's drill-down: each opens on what Today can't show (Click targets, above). Every place has the same three parts. Its reading is a headline, three tiles and at most five items, the rib's shape. Next come its threads with their status. Last is a composer anchored to it, so typing a question starts a thread there. The entitlements page from the first revision becomes the service reading, without "gate 3" (it reads "release review") and without the future "Upstream MRs" line. Its Threads section becomes the place's thread list and composer. Security is a place whose reading is SPI-owned findings across the fleet: tiles for high, total and upstream-origin counts, items grouped by rule, and each rule that spans forks shown as a detected campaign.

The Environment place

The Environment place leads with its own state, because the top row only says Ready or Maintenance and this is where you find out why. Its parts keep the place shape, in this order:

  • Three lead tiles from core's briefing.environment.facts. The first is Maintenance while a hold is open: the hold's length as its value and its start as "since 11-11 07:40", framed in the failed tone when nothing is running to clear it and in the warning tone when a lifecycle run is. Otherwise it is Running, with core's sub ("ready · green at 11-10 22:05"). Then the availability tile, "Ready · 21 days", and Last proof. With no environment facts, or a Held lane that wasn't read, the place draws the reading's own tiles and no Maintenance tile.
  • The last 24 hours, open. The timeline sits between the tiles and the items without a fold to open. An open hold is drawn red and open-ended; an ended one is muted. A lane with nothing in the day says "none in the last day" where it can be read.
  • The items, the reading's at most five, with the ones that ask for the operator grouped under "Waiting on you".
  • A quiet list after the items: Version, Fork deploys and Stack repository. Fork deploys reads "All passed", or a count of what didn't ("2 failed") with the forks named; it never says "Lane", "Stopped" or "n of m".
  • Stack runs stay a fold with the flow context, the run headline and the note, without the facts the tiles already show.

The reading's headline says the same state in a sentence: "v0.35.5 is ready, green since 11-10 22:05.", "v0.35.5 in maintenance for 2 d 13 h, with no run in flight.", or "v0.35.5 is in maintenance while an upgrade to v0.35.6 runs.". When core knows maintenance is set but not since when, it says "v0.35.5 is in maintenance with no run in flight." with no length. The window maps no words: what it draws is what core sends.

A fork check that failed on the environment and should be re-run lives with the fork, not here: its re-run lands on the fork's PR, so the item's scope is that fork and its title leads with the ask ("Re-run …") once the environment is ready. When several forks failed, it stays one item whose scope counts them.

Attention

Core assigns every state to one tier. The page doesn't decide what's loud.

TierWhat qualifiesHow it reaches you
InterruptA card waiting on your decision. A new or worsened P0. An owned item whose thread Failed, stalled or lost coverage. A failed recovery.   • Since you last looked. The seen state decides the "new" chips and whether the app opens on Today; the verdict is core's briefing.verdict, not a diff, and no row on Today lists what ran since the last look. Notifications use the same seen state, as T3 Code's background-thread announcer stays quiet on first sight and announces each run's end once. Acknowledging never clears a stopped station; the top row keeps counting it.
  • Ownership suppresses duplicates, not escalation. When a thread owns an item (TH-05), it keeps its home on Today with the thread's marker and its repeat notifications stop. If the owning thread fails, stalls, loses coverage, or the item worsens, the item interrupts again. A stopped fork stays in the top row's red count until the line is green; a thread finishing doesn't make the line healthy.
  • Due items escalate by tier, not by group. Today has no deadline group and the strip no due count. Inside its lead time, if nobody owns it, core makes an item an Interrupt, so it reaches Needs you, the Today count and a notification. A partition halt can't arrive unannounced because the app restored a thread instead of Today.
  • One count, of distinct items. The Today count is the number of Interrupt items: a pending card counts once, and the thread waiting on that card and the P0 it owns are not counted again. The count falls when an item is resolved, not when it's acknowledged. Acknowledging clears the notification and the open-on-Today trigger.
  • One home per tier. Interrupt in Needs you, including a pending card raised by a button, except a stop of the shared environment, which is drawn in Stops the line above Needs you and still counted in the Today count; Surface on its horizon card or in Decisions waiting; Ambient in Failing or In motion; Background in a place card's count or Can wait. Today never re-tiers an item to fit a group. The P0/P1 rank stays as the chip on each row.
  • Snooze until the next sync for a Surface item, borrowed from T3 Code's snooze dialog.

Growth

Every new capability declares four things, and that declaration replaces a new page or sidebar group:

  • Its anchor kind and reading, so it becomes a place with a headline, tiles and items.
  • Its attention rule, mapping each of its states to Interrupt, Surface or Ambient.
  • Whether it borrows the lane, so the lane queue and Lane watch cover it.
  • Whether it's fleet-scoped, in which case it runs as a campaign.

Text rules

  • Headlines state a verdict or a change. At most 12 words. Today's verdict is the exception: it keeps "all of it; it is the briefing's one sentence". The rib's "One filter entry would stop 4 future halts" is the model.
  • A word when fine, a count of what is not; never "n of m". A state reads Ready, Current, Flowing or All passed when nothing is wrong, and otherwise counts only what is wrong ("3 pending", "1 failed · 2 pending", "4 stopped", "2 failed"), with the names behind the count in its hover. The top row and the Environment place follow this; Today's place cards keep their figures.
  • Ready and Maintenance. Wherever the window words the environment's state it says Ready, not "deployable", and Maintenance, not "held". "Deployable" stays the name of core's check and of the bindings that wait on it, in Detail and Evidence.
  • An item lives where its action lands. A fork check to re-run after the environment failed it belongs to the fork, because the re-run lands on the fork's PR, and its title leads with the ask. State is shown once, on the surface that owns it.
  • Every measure names what it counts, and a summary never says more than its source: "1 stopped" doesn't become "12 moving", and "13 open" doesn't become "13 ready". The rib's "27 min" is a run duration that reads like proof age. "Stopped 16 days" next to "21h" needs both labels.
  • Causes need evidence. Overlap is "possible contention" until a log corroborates it; nothing is exonerated by timing alone.
  • One verb per action. "Fix in code thread", not "Open in inspector · Investigate, then Dispatch cascade".
  • No sentence repeats what a component draws, and fleet facts appear once, on the campaign.
  • Internal names stay in Detail and Evidence. That covers fork_upstream, "gate 3", "presses" and "cut". Raw issue-title emoji are stripped.
  • Absent features take no space. No "not available yet" sections, and no future lines in mockups or pages.

Decisions for you

  • Parity. Replace word-for-word parity with reachability, and hold t1k.59 and t1k.62 until the new layout lands.
  • Landing. Open on Today only when something that interrupts arrived since you last looked, and otherwise restore the last thread.
  • Standing orders. One choice is a bounded rule you approve once, such as "rerun environment-overlap failures once per run until Friday". That changes AU-03. The other is keeping one card per rerun and letting Lane watch make each card one click. This page assumes the second for v1.
  • Sequencing guard. Does the stack's workflow already stop two forks borrowing at once? If not, the guard is a template change.
  • Sidebar. Services leave the sidebar except when pinned. You reach them from the Line sheet, a place or ⌘K.
  • Agent defaults. Which model Lane watch, Diagnose and campaign threads use by default. A watcher that runs all day is a natural fit for a 0× model.
  • Due lead time. How long before a deadline an unowned item interrupts. This page uses 2 hours as a setting.
