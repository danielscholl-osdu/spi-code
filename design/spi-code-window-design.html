spi-code · window information design · council review, six rounds

SPI Code Factory Line

An agent workbench for the SPI program. Work happens in threads, the factory line stays in view as status marks on the sidebar rows and a sheet you pull up, and the window interrupts you only when a decision is yours.

The current window reads like a transcript of the sweep, and the first revision of this page made it a better dashboard. A service page shows about 520 words. The revised Today packed a 13 × 6 grid, three bands and a queue into one view, which is busier than the rib console it replaces. It still asked the operator to visit pages and coordinate the work mentally. Threads were annotations on those pages.

This revision makes the thread the place work happens. The window has three layers. The fixed Line, Environment and Template rows and the removable Upstream pin show the line's state, and the footer says when it was read. The centre holds Today, a place such as a service, or a thread. Outside a thread the window is the sidebar and the centre; a thread adds its own context pane on the right. Attention has four tiers decided by core, and only a decision that's yours interrupts. Watchers and campaign threads notice problems and draft the exact approval card. The operator decides, in one place per card.

Some things this doesn't change or provide. Every write still needs its own card (AU-03 to AU-10), and no agent approves anything. Lane occupancy, collision attribution, seen-state and due times are new data, marked where they appear. Sequencing across forks must be enforced in the stack's workflows, because the app can't stop jobs GitHub starts on its own.

Colors identify the part of the system

Template · osdu-spi templates and workflows, rolled out to forks Environment · the shared CI/CD stack every prove borrows Services · 13 syncing forks of upstream GitLab Releases · monthly release lines

The window

The window is T3 Code's shell. The sidebar holds work. The centre holds one thing: Today, a place or a thread. The right panel belongs to a thread; outside one there is no panel and no panel toggle. Today's rows open in place and a place's marks open a popover, so nothing about a selection needs a second column. A thread brings its context pane (M3). The fixed status block and the Upstream pin carry the program's state, and the footer carries the sweep on every surface, so the line is never more than a glance away.

The mockups on this page draw what each surface says, not how the window is laid out. Their fixed sidebar and thread-pane widths and their one bar across the top are superseded by the window layout: each pane owns its part of one 44 px top row, the pin control sits at the end of the centre's part and the sweep beside the footer gear, panes are flush and resize within a width budget, and Settings is a dialog behind the sidebar's gear. For layout, panes, the top row and Settings, the window layout wins over this page.

The top row and footer

The top row holds the place's title and chips, and a pinnable place's or service's icon control at the right end of the centre's part. It stays at every width; Today, release months and the fixed places have no control. The program's states sit on the sidebar rows that explain them.

  • The sweep is said once, beside the footer gear: a plain 28 px button with the time, muted when every area read; an amber alert when an area is stale, incomplete or cut, and a red alert when one failed; "Sweeping" or "First sweep" with a spinner while running. No count follows the time. Its sentence is its hover and accessible name. Clicking opens the source popover upward, with the next sweep and upstream sync, Sweep now and Evidence. While a sweep runs, a 2 px line stays under the whole top row, with the sidebar shown, hidden or off-canvas.
  • There are no service pips. A service is reached from the Line sheet, a place or ⌘K, and pinned with the same icon control on its place, Line row or Find result (PS-17).
  • The lane's holder and the waiting cards have no mark yet. In this design the window has no mark for the lane's holder when one is borrowing (M3) or the count of approval cards waiting (M4); spicode-cp0.1 decides where they go.

The window layout draws the footer sweep's states and popover and the pin control's end position as the centre narrows.

The sidebar

The sidebar holds where you go. Top to bottom: Find or ask (⌘K); a fixed block with no header, Today with its count, Line with ⌘L, Environment and Template; then one collapsible Pinned list in the operator's order, hidden when empty. Its footer has the Settings gear at the start and the sweep at the end, with the pin notice just above it.

Environment's check is green, its wrench amber and its refresh icon in the work tone; Upstream's clock is in the upstream ink. There is no red Environment state. The tones hold in both themes and both palettes.
  • Nothing to say when fine. A fine Line or Template draws nothing; only Environment shows a check, because a ready environment is what every prove needs. A field core couldn't read draws nothing too: the environment's figure unread, the rollout's slots unknown, or the forks' stations unread, empty or missing. Nothing unread is drawn as a check.
  • Hover and the accessible name carry the sentence behind the mark, a fine Environment's included, and name the forks behind a count ("Stopped: storage, indexer-queue"); Template also names the target revision. The maintenance age is the open hold's, from the Held lane core sends. The fixed rows keep their marks, and Upstream keeps its clock wherever the operator moves its pin; an unpinned Upstream draws its clock nowhere.
  • The fixed block stays put. Today, Line, Environment and Template have no grip, context menu or pin control anywhere, including Find, and never reorder. Pinned holds only removable pins: Upstream, Releases and Security on first run, then what the operator pins. Its order and the operator's removed seed places survive a restart.
  • Line opens the sheet, as ⌘L does. The sheet's toolbar says Dock or Undock with a panel icon; docking keeps it beside the centre and never touches the sidebar.
  • One control pins from where a thing is found. A hollow pin means unpinned, a filled pin means pinned, and a filled control shows pin-off on hover or focus. It sits at the top row's right end on a place or service, at a Find result's end, and immediately after a service's name on the Line sheet; the sheet's row menu has Open, then Pin to sidebar or Unpin from sidebar. The control's action name and title name the thing. After a click its new glyph stays until pointer or focus leaves. ⌘⇧P (Ctrl+Shift+P) toggles the open place or service, or Find's highlighted result. It does nothing on Today, a release month or a fixed place, under another dialog or while Line overlays the centre. ⌘↩ on a Find result opens and pins, never unpins, and a fixed result simply opens. Clicking Find's control toggles in place, with the palette, query, highlight and focus unchanged.
  • A pinned service carries a word, and only when it is read and not fine: "stopped" in red, "waiting" in amber. Releases and Security carry nothing; Today and their places say how they stand.
  • Each row keeps its icon, label and status. Icons use the visual design's muted line set; only Upstream's clock uses upstream ink. Hover and focus add the background and, on Pinned rows, the grip; the mark or word never hides, moves or swaps. Keyboard focus adds a ring. A Pinned row's context menu (right click, long press, Shift-F10 or Menu key) has Open, Move up, Move down, a separator and Unpin from sidebar. Alt+Up and Alt+Down move a focused pin. Drag reorders within Pinned with an insertion line, and a drop on the fixed block or outside does nothing. After Unpin, focus goes next, else previous, else the Pinned header, else the fixed block's last row.
  • Today's count is what is yours. It counts the members on Today's Blocked and Waiting on you, 18 on 16 Nov, and each place's head counts its own share of them, so the place counts add up to it (Places, below). It falls when a member leaves Today, not when it is acknowledged.
  • A hidden sidebar still says something needs you. Its toggle carries one dot: error when an Interrupt is open (Attention, below), a status mark is error or the sweep is bad; otherwise warning when a mark is warning or the sweep is warn. Error wins across sources; an unread sweep adds nothing. A collapsed Pinned header reads only its rows' marks and service words, stopped in error and waiting in warning; no word read gives no dot.
  • Every unpin offers Undo. "Unpinned partition · Undo" lasts 8 s, in the band above the footer or at the window's bottom left while the sidebar is hidden or off-canvas. Undo or ⌘Z (Ctrl+Z) restores the old position. A pin appends and flashes the new row's accent background for about 1 s when Pinned is visible and open; otherwise it offers "Pinned partition · Undo", whose Undo unpins. The newest notice replaces the last. The status region starts empty so it is announced, Undo is keyboard reachable, and hover or focus pauses the timer. A shown notice owns ⌘Z inside Find too, until another key; normal Undo works elsewhere. Changes and Undo update this window even if storage refuses a write: the notice names the change without claiming it was saved, and reload reads the last list storage accepted.
  • The gear takes a dot only when Readiness is known to need the operator. Ready, or not yet checked, shows none. ⌘, opens the same dialog.

Threads join the sidebar under Pinned at M3, in shelves by status; the shell around them does not move.

Today

Today is a to-do list, not a summary: what is yours to move today, as a table. State lives on the sidebar's rows and on the places, and Today says nothing they already say. Today and its places holds the settled anatomy with every ruling behind it, and its mock draws it from the live sweep of 16 Nov; this section is the summary the rest of this page builds on.

Top to bottom: the date, which is the whole lede; a caveat band when a source's read failed or was cut; then five sections in one row grid. Today lifts the centre's 960 px content cap and uses the full centre width; the window layout states the exception.

  • Rows. Fork, What, Open, Age. A single item is one row: its fork, a short headline in the operator's words, its source pill and its age. A group is a header row ("5 forks", the shared headline, the oldest age) and one sub-row per member with its fork, its pill and its own age, so every link sits in one column and every age in another. A member's age is the age of the object you act on: unit's is its sync PR's 10d, not the 59d its upstream change has been queued.
  • No verbs and no restated facts. Core's verb names (Classify entry, Investigate, Re-run lanes) never show; lines group by situation, not by verb. A line doesn't repeat its age, its link, a rank or what its section already says, and the lower three sections are counts, not sentences. There are no P0 or P1 chips.
  • Links are typed pills, an icon with the kind and number: PR in teal, Issue in blue, a GitLab MR in the upstream colour, Commit, Run and Deployments in grey. A pill opens its source in the browser and does nothing else.
  • A deadline is a clock by the age, with its reason on hover and focus ("Before the next upstream sync, Tue 18:00"). A condition such as "after lanes drain" is not a deadline; it is the first step in the opened row.
  • ⌀ n by the age says SPI Code didn't read n things behind the row. It opens Evidence for that row. A member shows ⌀ only for a gap its group doesn't already mark.
  • Three grouping rules, all core's. Whose move decides Today: an item is on Today when its next move is yours, whatever its tier. One member per object: two items on the same PR are one member, whose opened row names both moves in order (upgrade os-core-common on the fork, then review the sync PR). A blocked item's move is its blocker's: the five red release/upstream PRs failed on their deploy lanes, so their move is the lanes' re-run and they group under one headline. On 16 Nov this turns 23 items into 10 rows and 18 members: 2 Blocked and 16 Waiting on you.
  • A row opens in place. Clicking a row's fork or headline expands it under the row, across What to Age: the place link (Line › fork › station), the deadline in full, the cause as its first sentence, the effect as a muted second one (dropped when nothing waits on it), the steps as a numbered list only when there are two, the source pills, and "⌀ n not read · Evidence ›" when something wasn't read. There are no field labels. A group opens under its header with the shared cause and steps; its members are already the sub-rows, so the body carries no single member's pill, and a pill about one member names its fork. A sub-row opens under itself. The opened row never repeats the pill in its own Open column. One row is open at a time; clicking it again, Esc or a click elsewhere closes it.
  • No action buttons yet. A pill is the way out to a source, and the first pill is the primary one, which ⌘↵ opens. An operation only SPI Code can run ("Queue the re-run of all 5 lanes", "Dispatch Sync Template to 7 forks") needs a request the window can send, which M4 Act brings; until then it takes no space and its step reads as a sentence. What the operator does by hand is a sentence naming the file or MR it touches. How SPI Code works with Copilot from a row is a separate design.
  • The lower three sections open their place. Their rows have nothing to open in place, so the whole row opens its place, with › at the end and the place's name on hover; a Line row opens the Line sheet.

From upstream

Everything on Today is either in your system (the SPI forks, their PRs, the environment, the template and the releases) or comes from upstream GitLab (commits queued for the next sync or held in open sync PRs, and merged provider changes not yet ported). Upstream origin has one owner colour, the visual design's upstream role, and one inbound glyph, ⇣. They mark GitLab MR pills, the Upstream place and its marks, and nothing else; colour is never the only signal. Today has no horizon block. An upstream change that needs you is a row like any other, whose home is Upstream unless a fork's sync PR already carries it, in which case it is a member on the Line. What is coming at the next sync is read on Upstream.

Words and visuals

Today is read at a glance, not read through. A word stays only when it does work: the fork, the headline, the pill's kind and number, the age. Everything else is a mark whose sentence is its hover and accessible name: the section's dot, the clock, ⌀ n. The opened row and the popover follow the same rule. Grouping, headlines, steps and deadlines are core's output; the window draws them and computes nothing beyond layout.

Two glyph sets are still drawn, each with one meaning everywhere it appears. The Line sheet's stations use ✕ stopped, ! waits on you, ◐ running, ◷ waits on time, ⌀ not read and · fine (The Line sheet, below). Template's rollout ladder gives each fork one stage: verified, merged · verification pending, to dispatch, failed or not read. Merged is read only from Sync Template's own merge results, so a result that wasn't read says not read, never merged.

Narrow widths

Today is the same table at every width. When the centre is under 640 px the Fork, Open and Age columns narrow and What keeps the rest. Nothing folds, no group shows "n more", and an opened row still spans What to Age. A long pill label ends in an ellipsis with the full label in its title. At 740 px the sidebar goes off-canvas and the table keeps the whole centre.

The bad morning

More rows under Blocked and Waiting on you, and GitHub failed to read.

  • A caveat band, full width under the date, with one line for each area whose read failed or was cut: "GitHub issues and PRs read failed at 08:15; items from it may be missing." or "GitHub issues and PRs was read only in part at 08:15; items from it may be missing." The area's name is the link: it opens Evidence, and there is no separate Evidence action. An area that was only not read this sweep adds no line. The band adds "Open Settings ›" only when a failure's code is one Readiness fixes, such as signin.
  • A row SPI Code couldn't fully read carries ⌀ n by its age, and its opened row ends "⌀ n not read · Evidence ›".
  • A pile or workflow core didn't read says "not read", with ⌀ n opening Evidence, never 0. A pile that was read and holds nothing says none.
  • The sidebar rows and footer behave as they do on every morning: a row whose source wasn't read carries no mark, never a check, and the sweep takes the red alert icon for the failed read.

What goes

  • The briefing that Today used to be: the verdict sentence, the attention band with Stops the line, Needs you, Decisions waiting and Failing, the upstream horizon, the four place cards, the Releases block and In motion. Their items are rows; their state is on the sidebar rows and the places.
  • The P0, P1 and P2 rank chips, and every "n more" fold.
  • A second column for the selection outside a thread. A row opens in place, a place's mark opens a popover, and Evidence is a page.
  • The composer at the foot of Today. Starting a thread from Today waits for the Copilot design.
  • Today's three tiles and the 75-word and five-item caps.
  • The deadline groups (Before the next sync) and the New group. A deadline is a clock on its row.
  • The old top row's "n due before 18:00" field. The Upstream pin shows the next upstream sync only within 2 hours of it, as a clock.
  • The old top row's service pips and the stopped names. The Line row carries a count; the names are in its hover and on the Line sheet.
  • Release months on Today. Release history stays on Releases.

Before the first sweep finishes there is nothing to list yet. Today shows the sweep's own sources live from sweep.progress, in sweep order and in Evidence words, each with what it reads and its state, with no closing line under the list, and hands over to the table in place when view.snapshot lands; if the window is hidden then, one "Today is ready" notification says so. Places open one line saying when they can be read, the pins carry no marks, and the footer sweep shows "First sweep" with a spinner. If the first sweep stops, Today keeps the list and offers Try again and Open Settings. The window layout's first-sweep frame draws it.

Places

A place is any anchor you can open: a service, the environment, Template, a release month, a campaign. Places are Today's drill-down: each opens on what Today can't show (Click targets, above). Line, Environment and Template stay in the fixed block; Upstream, Releases and Security seed the removable pins, and a service joins Pinned when the operator pins it (The sidebar, above). Every place has the same three parts. Its reading is a headline, three tiles and at most five items, the rib's shape. Next come its threads with their status. Last is a composer anchored to it, so typing a question starts a thread there. The entitlements page from the first revision becomes the service reading, without "gate 3" (it reads "release review") and without the future "Upstream MRs" line. Its Threads section becomes the place's thread list and composer. Security is a place whose reading is SPI-owned findings across the fleet: tiles for high, total and upstream-origin counts, items grouped by rule, and each rule that spans forks shown as a detected campaign.

The Environment place

The Environment place leads with its own state, because its fixed row only shows a check, a wrench or the refresh icon and this is where you find out why. Its parts keep the place shape, in this order:

  • Three lead tiles from core's briefing.environment.facts. The first is Maintenance while a hold is open: the hold's length as its value and its start as "since 11-11 07:40", framed in the failed tone when nothing is running to clear it and in the warning tone when a lifecycle run is. Otherwise it is Running, with core's sub ("ready · green at 11-10 22:05"). Then the availability tile, "Ready · 21 days", and Last proof. With no environment facts, or a Held lane that wasn't read, the place draws the reading's own tiles and no Maintenance tile.
  • The last 24 hours, open. The timeline sits between the tiles and the items without a fold to open. An open hold is drawn red and open-ended; an ended one is muted. A lane with nothing in the day says "none in the last day" where it can be read.
  • The items, the reading's at most five, with the ones that ask for the operator grouped under "Waiting on you".
  • A quiet list after the items: Version, Fork deploys and Stack repository. Fork deploys reads "All passed", or a count of what didn't ("2 failed") with the forks named; it never says "Lane", "Stopped" or "n of m".
  • Stack runs stay a fold with the flow context, the run headline and the note, without the facts the tiles already show.

The reading's headline says the same state in a sentence: "v0.35.5 is ready, green since 11-10 22:05.", "v0.35.5 in maintenance for 2 d 13 h, with no run in flight.", or "v0.35.5 is in maintenance while an upgrade to v0.35.6 runs.". When core knows maintenance is set but not since when, it says "v0.35.5 is in maintenance with no run in flight." with no length. The window maps no words: what it draws is what core sends.

A fork check that failed on the environment and should be re-run lives with the fork, not here: its re-run lands on the fork's PR, so the item's scope is that fork and its title leads with the ask ("Re-run …") once the environment is ready. When several forks failed, it stays one item whose scope counts them.

Attention

Core assigns every state to one tier. The page doesn't decide what's loud.

TierWhat qualifiesHow it reaches you
InterruptA card waiting on your decision. A new or worsened P0. An owned item whose thread Failed, stalled or lost coverage. A failed recovery. An unowned item that is due within its lead time (2 hours by default).One OS notification per item, the dock badge and a hidden sidebar's red dot. If it arrived since you last acknowledged, the app opens on Today, where it is a row when its next move is yours.
SurfaceA new P1, a newly detected theme, a thread Done, an item due before the next sync but outside its lead timeIts row on Today when its next move is yours. No notification.
AmbientWork moving normally, successful retries, routine sweeps, no-rush itemsThe status rows' marks, the Line sheet and its place; Moving without you or Last 24 hours on Today when it is in motion or has run
BackgroundWork that asks nothing of you todayCan wait's piles, or its place. No notification.
  • Since you last looked. The seen state decides whether the app opens on Today, and Today's last section reads "Since you last looked" only when core has a seen cursor; without one it reads "Last 24 hours", or "Since 20:00" or "24 hours to 22:00" when core's history is short or stale. Notifications use the same seen state, as T3 Code's background-thread announcer stays quiet on first sight and announces each run's end once. Acknowledging never clears a stopped station; the Line row keeps counting it.
  • Ownership suppresses duplicates, not escalation. When a thread owns an item (TH-05), it keeps its home on Today with the thread's marker and its repeat notifications stop. If the owning thread fails, stalls, loses coverage, or the item worsens, the item interrupts again. A stopped fork stays in the Line row's red count until the line is green; a thread finishing doesn't make the line healthy.
  • Due items escalate by tier, not by group. Today has no deadline group and the top row no due count. Inside its lead time, if nobody owns it, core makes an item an Interrupt, so it notifies and reaches the dock badge; on Today its row carries the clock either way. A partition halt can't arrive unannounced because the app restored a thread instead of Today.
  • Two counts, one open question. Today's count on the sidebar is the members on Blocked and Waiting on you, and falls when a member leaves Today, not when it's acknowledged. Notifications, the dock badge, the tray and a hidden sidebar's dot count distinct Interrupts: a pending card counts once, and the thread waiting on it and the P0 it owns are not counted again. Whether they should count the same members as the sidebar is open, for the checkpoint on the packaged window (spicode-djg.29). Acknowledging clears the notification and the open-on-Today trigger.
  • Whose move decides Today, not the tier. The tier decides how loud an item is, not where it sits. An item is on Today when its next move is yours, whatever its tier: under Blocked when it stops something until you act (core's rung 0), otherwise under Waiting on you. Work whose move isn't yours is counted under Can wait, shown under Moving without you or Last 24 hours, or marked at its place. Today never re-tiers an item, and draws no rank chip.
  • Snooze until the next sync for a Surface item, borrowed from T3 Code's snooze dialog.

Growth

Every new capability declares four things, and that declaration replaces a new page or sidebar group:

  • Its anchor kind and reading, so it becomes a place with a headline, tiles and items.
  • Its attention rule, mapping each of its states to Interrupt, Surface or Ambient.
  • Whether it borrows the lane, so the lane queue and Lane watch cover it.
  • Whether it's fleet-scoped, in which case it runs as a campaign.

Text rules

  • No word that doesn't do work. Today shows no verb, no field label, no restated age, link or rank, and no explanation under a heading; an opened row or a popover says only what its pills and its place don't (Today and its places, X1, X3, X11).
  • Headlines state a situation or a change. At most 12 words, in the operator's words: the fork and what is wrong ("crs-conversion sync halted"). The rib's "One filter entry would stop 4 future halts" is the model.
  • Nothing to say when fine, a count of what is not; never "n of m". A status row shows nothing when its count is fine and otherwise its worst count, outlined, with the count's words in its hover ("3 pending", "1 failed", "4 stopped"); a place's list reads All passed when nothing is wrong and otherwise counts only what is wrong ("2 failed"). The names behind a count are in its hover. The sidebar's status rows and the Environment place follow this.
  • Ready and Maintenance. Wherever the window words the environment's state, in hovers, headlines and the Environment place, it says Ready, not "deployable", and Maintenance, not "held". "Deployable" stays the name of core's check and of the bindings that wait on it, in Evidence.
  • An item lives where its action lands. A fork check to re-run after the environment failed it belongs to the fork, because the re-run lands on the fork's PR, and its title leads with the ask. State is shown once, on the surface that owns it.
  • Every measure names what it counts, and a summary never says more than its source: "1 stopped" doesn't become "12 moving", and "13 open" doesn't become "13 ready". The rib's "27 min" is a run duration that reads like proof age. "Stopped 16 days" next to "21h" needs both labels.
  • Causes need evidence. Overlap is "possible contention" until a log corroborates it; nothing is exonerated by timing alone.
  • One verb per action. "Fix in code thread", not "Open in inspector · Investigate, then Dispatch cascade".
  • No sentence repeats what a component draws, and fleet facts appear once, on the campaign.
  • Internal names stay in Evidence. That covers fork_upstream, "gate 3", "presses" and "cut". Core's verb names and error codes (UNKNOWN_TOP_LEVEL) never reach the operator. Raw issue-title emoji are stripped.
  • Absent features take no space. No "not available yet" sections, and no future lines in mockups or pages.

Decisions for you

  • Parity. Replace word-for-word parity with reachability, and hold t1k.59 and t1k.62 until the new layout lands.
  • Landing. Open on Today only when something that interrupts arrived since you last looked, and otherwise restore the last thread.
  • Standing orders. One choice is a bounded rule you approve once, such as "rerun environment-overlap failures once per run until Friday". That changes AU-03. The other is keeping one card per rerun and letting Lane watch make each card one click. This page assumes the second for v1.
  • Sequencing guard. Does the stack's workflow already stop two forks borrowing at once? If not, the guard is a template change.
  • Sidebar. Services leave the sidebar except when pinned. You reach them from the Line sheet, a place or ⌘K.
  • Agent defaults. Which model Lane watch, Diagnose and campaign threads use by default. A watcher that runs all day is a natural fit for a 0× model.
  • Due lead time. How long before a deadline an unowned item interrupts. This page uses 2 hours as a setting.
