spi-code · window information design · council review, six rounds

SPI Code Factory Line

An agent workbench for the SPI program. Work happens in threads, the factory line stays in view as a strip and a sheet you pull up, and the window interrupts you only when a decision is yours.

The current window reads like a transcript of the sweep, and the first revision of this page made it a better dashboard. A service page shows about 520 words. The revised Today packed a 13 × 6 grid, three bands and a queue into one view, which is busier than the rib console it replaces. It still asked the operator to visit pages and coordinate the work mentally. Threads were annotations on those pages.

This revision makes the thread the place work happens. The window has three layers. A strip under the title bar always shows the line's state. The centre holds either a short brief (Today), a place such as a service, or a thread. The right panel holds context for whatever the centre shows. Attention has three tiers decided by core, and only a decision that's yours interrupts. Watchers and campaign threads notice problems and draft the exact approval card. The operator decides, in one place per card.

Some things this doesn't change or provide. Every write still needs its own card (AU-03 to AU-10), and no agent approves anything. Lane occupancy, collision attribution, seen-state and due times are new data, marked where they appear. Sequencing across forks must be enforced in the stack's workflows, because the app can't stop jobs GitHub starts on its own.

Places

A place is any anchor you can open: a service, the environment, the engineering system, a release month, a campaign. Every place has the same three parts. Its reading is a headline, three tiles and at most five items, the rib's shape. Next come its threads with their status. Last is a composer anchored to it, so typing a question starts a thread there. The entitlements page from the first revision becomes the service reading, without "gate 3" (it reads "release review") and without the future "Upstream MRs" line. Its Threads section becomes the place's thread list and composer. Security is a place whose reading is SPI-owned findings across the fleet: tiles for high, total and upstream-origin counts, items grouped by rule, and each rule that spans forks shown as a detected campaign.

Upstream

The line has an input, the machinery and an output. Upstream is the input: what GitLab will hand each fork at the next sync. It gathers what was spread across four spots: the strip's next sync, Today's Before next sync group, each service's commits incoming and the Line sheet's Upstream column. Today keeps the part of it that has a deadline. Upstream is where you go to look further ahead, and its toolchain moves are what campaign detection reads.

Attention

Core assigns every state to one tier. The page doesn't decide what's loud.

TierWhat qualifiesHow it reaches you
InterruptA card waiting on your decision. A new or worsened P0. An owned item whose thread Failed, stalled or lost coverage. A failed recovery. An unowned item that is due within its lead time (2 hours by default).One OS notification per item, the Today count, and the strip. If it arrived since you last acknowledged, the app opens on Today.
SurfaceA new P1, a newly detected theme, a thread Done, an item due before the next sync but outside its lead timeToday's brief, marked new, and the strip's due count. No notification.
AmbientWork moving normally, successful retries, routine sweeps, no-rush itemsThe strip and the Line sheet only
  • Since you last looked. Today's headline is a diff against the state you last acknowledged, as T3 Code's background-thread announcer stays quiet on first sight and announces each run's end once. Acknowledging never clears a stopped station; the strip keeps showing it.
  • Ownership suppresses duplicates, not escalation. When a thread owns an item (TH-05), it moves to Today's Owned group and its repeat notifications stop. If the owning thread fails, stalls, loses coverage, or the item worsens, the item interrupts again. A P0 stays red on the strip until the line is green; a thread finishing doesn't make the line healthy.
  • Due items escalate. An item with a due time sits in Before the next sync, and the strip counts it ("2 due before 18:00"). Inside its lead time, if nobody owns it, it becomes Interrupt. A partition halt can't arrive unannounced because the app restored a thread instead of Today.
  • One count, of distinct items. The Today count is the number of Interrupt items: a pending card counts once, and the thread waiting on that card and the P0 it owns are not counted again. The count falls when an item is resolved, not when it's acknowledged. Acknowledging clears the notification and the open-on-Today trigger.
  • Grouped by when it bites. Waiting for you (only when a card raised by a button is pending), Before the next sync, Owned, New, then one folded line for the rest. The P0/P1 rank stays as the chip on each row.
  • Snooze until the next sync for a Surface item, borrowed from T3 Code's snooze dialog.

Agents make two things cheap: noticing, and drafting the exact card. Deciding stays with you.

  • Approved is not executed. A card bound to a wait reads approved, waiting until the wait fires. The host then re-reads its bindings (AU-06). If one changed, the card moves to binding failed and names it.
  • Core admits work to the lane. Waves from every campaign, reruns and ordinary proves enter one admission queue, ordered by core's pacing and ordering guards and capped by lane capacity. These are the bindings Consequential calls already re-read. Lane watch reports on the queue but admits nothing. The stack's workflow guard enforces the same order for jobs the app didn't start.

Growth

Every new capability declares four things, and that declaration replaces a new page or sidebar group:

  • Its anchor kind and reading, so it becomes a place with a headline, tiles and items.
  • Its attention rule, mapping each of its states to Interrupt, Surface or Ambient.
  • Whether it borrows the lane, so the lane queue and Lane watch cover it.
  • Whether it's fleet-scoped, in which case it runs as a campaign.

Text rules

  • Headlines state a verdict or a change. At most 12 words. The rib's "One filter entry would stop 4 future halts" is the model.
  • Every measure names what it counts, and a summary never says more than its source: "1 of 13 stopped" doesn't become "12 moving", and "13 open" doesn't become "13 ready". The rib's "27 min" is a run duration that reads like proof age. "Stopped 16 days" next to "21h" needs both labels.
  • Causes need evidence. Overlap is "possible contention" until a log corroborates it; nothing is exonerated by timing alone.
  • One verb per action. "Fix in code thread", not "Open in inspector · Investigate, then Dispatch cascade".
  • No sentence repeats what a component draws, and fleet facts appear once, on the campaign.
  • Internal names stay in Detail and Evidence. That covers fork_upstream, "gate 3", "presses" and "cut". Raw issue-title emoji are stripped.
  • Absent features take no space. No "not available yet" sections, and no future lines in mockups or pages.

Decisions for you

  • Parity. Replace word-for-word parity with reachability, and hold t1k.59 and t1k.62 until the new layout lands.
  • Landing. Open on Today only when something that interrupts arrived since you last looked, and otherwise restore the last thread.
  • Standing orders. One choice is a bounded rule you approve once, such as "rerun environment-overlap failures once per run until Friday". That changes AU-03. The other is keeping one card per rerun and letting Lane watch make each card one click. This page assumes the second for v1.
  • Sequencing guard. Does the stack's workflow already stop two forks borrowing at once? If not, the guard is a template change.
  • Sidebar. Services leave the sidebar except when pinned. You reach them from the strip, the Line sheet or ⌘K.
  • Agent defaults. Which model Lane watch, Diagnose and campaign threads use by default. A watcher that runs all day is a natural fit for a 0× model.
  • Due lead time. How long before a deadline an unowned item interrupts. This page uses 2 hours as a setting.
